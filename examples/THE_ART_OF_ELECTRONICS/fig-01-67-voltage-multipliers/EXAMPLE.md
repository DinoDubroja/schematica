## What it demonstrates

Three vertically stacked circuits compare A, doubler; B, tripler; and C, quadrupler. Each has a transformer on the left, diode/capacitor circuitry in the middle, and an exposed output terminal and dashed load on the right. A split bracket-like annotation beneath each circuit places the circuit name between horizontal lines with angled outer ends. The panel letter sits at the lower left.

The transformer windings and core are drawn explicitly. Polarized capacitors combine one straight and one curved plate with a nearby plus sign. Filled junction dots and hollow output terminals have different roles. Three-bar ground symbols repeat locally. The load rectangle, its connecting wires, and its ground indication use dashed strokes; the load label is rotated vertically. Circuit details and polarity markings differ between panels and must be read from the image rather than inferred from the repeated layout.

## Inferred lessons to review

- Consistent placement of the input, circuit, and load makes related topologies easier to compare.
- A short name inside a bracket-like annotation identifies a whole circuit without adding a surrounding box.
- Dashed load graphics distinguish that external element from the solid circuit drawing in this figure; this is not a confirmed convention for every load.
- Capacitor curvature and polarity marks, transformer geometry, and local ground placement provide additional AOE symbol references.

These are observations, not newly accepted general preferences or fixed style parameters. They do not select a global GND symbol or authorize changing existing drawings.
